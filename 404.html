05 Process

How I approach a difficult problem

A practical support and engineering loop: establish the facts, fix the cause, and leave the next person with a clearer system.

  1. 01

    Reproduce the issue

    Capture the environment, expected behavior, actual behavior, error output, and smallest reliable reproduction before changing anything.

  2. 02

    Isolate the cause

    Separate symptoms from causes, compare working and failing paths, inspect logs and tests, and state what is known versus inferred.

  3. 03

    Make the narrowest sound fix

    Correct the underlying behavior without hiding the failure, loosening a meaningful gate, or adding unrelated changes.

  4. 04

    Verify and document

    Run the relevant checks, confirm the result across affected environments, record limitations, and give the customer or next engineer a clear handoff.

06 How we work

Principles that do not change by role or project

  • Stay with the problem. I do not lose interest at the demo. Support, documentation, verification, and the unglamorous closing work are part of delivery.
  • Refuse rather than guess. If a payment, external action, or customer-facing response cannot be verified, the safer system stops and asks a person.
  • Say what is not ready. Unavailable integrations stay visibly disabled instead of being simulated as working. Limitations belong in the documentation.
  • Protect the customer relationship. Clear expectations, honest status, patient explanations, and reliable follow-through matter as much as technical range.

The through-line in my work is simple: turn ambiguous reports into reproducible facts, working software, and documentation another person can use.
