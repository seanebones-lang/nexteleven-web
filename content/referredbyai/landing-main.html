The problem

The AI answers with names. Either yours is one of them, or a competitor’s is.

More customers now skip the search page and ask ChatGPT, the AI answer at the top of Google, or Perplexity: “who’s a good shop near me?” The AI doesn’t hand them a list of links — it names businesses. Most shop owners have never seen whether their shop is one of the names, or who got picked instead. That’s what we measure, and then fix.

How it works

Ask. Measure. Fix. Re-measure.

One check, run the same way every time — so a score this month means the same thing next month.

STEP 1

Ask

We put the 8 questions your customers actually ask — best shop in town, brake repair, transmission work, oil change, A/C repair, open Saturday, shop with good reviews, price questions — to ChatGPT, Google’s AI, and Perplexity. 23 real AI answers, collected live.

STEP 2

Measure

Your AI Score: named in how many of the 23 answers, per engine — next to the leaderboard of who beat you, why they won, and your fix list in priority order. Every answer is in the back of the report as receipts.

STEP 3

Fix

On the managed plan, we do the work the audit points at: the website pages and leftovers, the listings made to match everywhere, and a consistent review-request process offered to eligible customers without filtering for positive feedback.

STEP 4

Re-measure

Every month we re-run the same 8 questions on all three AIs and send your new score next to last month’s. You watch the number move, in writing — a scoreboard, not a sales pitch.

The audit

What the audit contains

A written report, built from one live run of the 8-question check across Google’s AI, ChatGPT, and Perplexity.

  • Your AI Score, per engine

    How many of the 23 answers name your shop on ChatGPT, on Google’s AI, and on Perplexity — because the three don’t agree, and the fix differs by engine.

  • The leaderboard

    Every shop the AIs named instead of you, and how often. You see exactly who is getting the customers the AI sends out.

  • Why the winners win

    No engine publishes a formula, so the report shows the pattern in the answers, engine by engine. Perplexity said of its own first pick: “the strongest combination of rating and review volume.” The AIs also cite shops’ own service pages again and again.

  • Your fix list, in priority order

    The specific things hurting your score — a placeholder phone number still sitting in a website footer, listings that don’t match from site to site, the service pages the AIs look for and can’t find — ranked by what moves the needle first.

  • The full receipts

    Every one of the 23 answers, question by question, in the appendix. Nothing in the front of the report that you can’t check in the back.

  • A dated baseline

    Your score is a measurement from the day the check ran — a starting line to measure against, not a permanent ranking. The managed plan re-runs the identical check monthly so change is real change, not noise.

Questions

Straight answers

Can you guarantee the AI will name us?

No. And run from anyone who does. When Google changed the model behind its AI answers in January 2026, about 42% of previously cited domains changed in an SE Ranking study. That is a measurement of cited sources, not a claim that 42% of businesses disappeared. Nobody controls that — not us, not the expensive agencies. What we can do is the work the evidence points to, and show you a monthly scoreboard so you can see whether it’s paying off.

How do the AIs decide who to name?

No engine publishes a formula. In the answers themselves, the pattern is plain: rating combined with review count, and what your own website and listings say. Perplexity described its own first pick as “the strongest combination of rating and review volume,” and in our Mansfield run the most-named shop had 4.9 stars and 808 reviews, while the AIs kept citing shops’ own service pages. That’s why the managed plan is fixes, listings, and reviews — not tricks.

Why 23 answers?

Eight questions your customers ask, put to three AIs. Google’s AI doesn’t answer every question, so a full run produces 23 answers. Same 8 questions every month, so your scoreboard compares like with like.

Do I need a new website for this?

Not necessarily. Some fixes are website fixes — one shop in our test still had a placeholder phone number in its own footer, on the very page the AI was reading. Some are listings and reviews. The audit tells you which ones you actually need before you spend on any of them.

What do you need from me?

One short conversation — your shop’s name, services, hours, and where you’re listed. We do the asking, the checking, and the writing.

Can I just do this myself?

You can ask the AIs the questions yourself, and you should — it’s your score. What you’re paying for is the fix work and the steady monthly discipline: listings matched, pages written, reviews asked for at the right moment, and someone accountable for the number. That’s Sean, here in Mansfield, with his name and phone number on the report.
