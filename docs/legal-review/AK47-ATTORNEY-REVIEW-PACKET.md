## Questions for counsel

1. Does the product, purchase flow, documentation, or support model create adviser,
   broker, commodity trading adviser, exchange, money-transmission, solicitation, or
   other registration concerns under applicable federal or state law?
2. What exact “not advice,” “not a managed account,” customer-control, and risk
   language belongs on the public page, order, onboarding, and Telegram channel?
3. Are event contracts and prediction-market references described precisely enough,
   including jurisdiction, eligibility, venue, fee, settlement, and total-loss risk?
4. Can historical alerts, outcomes, running session figures, or screenshots be shown?
   If so, what methodology, time window, cost assumptions, record completeness, and
   prominence of limitations are required?
5. What license grant and restrictions are enforceable and appropriate? Clarify
   transfer, modification, reverse engineering, third-party credentials, venue terms,
   account ownership, and customer responsibility.
6. What refund, cancellation, acceptance, delivery, activation, warranty disclaimer,
   limitation-of-liability, indemnity, and dispute terms should control? Identify any
   consumer-law limits on “final sale” or similar wording.
7. Which terms must be conspicuous before payment, separately acknowledged, versioned,
   and retained with evidence of assent?
8. What privacy notice, data-processing disclosure, retention schedule, security
   statement, and incident language is required for leads, buyers, credentials,
   telemetry, market data, and support records?
9. Should the offer be limited by customer type, location, venue eligibility,
   sophistication, or acknowledgment of independent professional advice?
10. Which public statements should remain prohibited even after the agreement is
    revised?
